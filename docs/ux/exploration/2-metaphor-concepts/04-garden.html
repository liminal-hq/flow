FLOW / GARDEN

Work as something alive. Every focus is a plant: attention is water, thoughts are dew, tangents are literal branches. Neglected work wilts — visibly, kindly — and finished work blossoms and is carried to the meadow. You don't manage this garden. You tend it.

1

Attention is water. The plant you're tending is the one in bloom — it glows, fireflies gather, and every thought you type lands on it as dew. Click any other plant to move the watering can; the garden allows exactly one bloom at a time.

2

Wilting is information, not shame. Untouched work droops by degrees — thirsty, then dormant, then quietly composted into the archive. A drooping plant is the softest possible nudge, and "3 days without rain" reads very differently from "OVERDUE".

3

Done work blossoms. Finishing something is a visible, slightly celebratory event: the plant flowers, drops petals, and drifts off to the meadow — a separate scene of everything you've ever completed. The meadow is the anti-backlog: a place you visit to feel good.

4

Tangents are literal branches. A side-quest grows as a blue offshoot of the parent stem, with its own leaves and dew. Finish it and it fuses back into the stem's growth; abandon it and just that branch wilts, never the whole plant.

5

Roots are memory. Below the soil line, every plant's history — each pickup, pause and thought — grows downward as roots. Old work has deep roots you can excavate by scrolling down. Nothing is ever lost; it just goes underground.

6

Weather and seasons. The sky reflects load: clear when the garden is small, overcast when too much blooms at once. The season view compresses months into growth rings — your year, readable at a glance like a tree stump.
