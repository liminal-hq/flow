1

Now is furniture, not a row. The active focus is a fixed block above the list — visually a different species from queue items, so "what am I doing" and "what's next" can never blur. Its three verbs (done, set aside, sub-task) are the only buttons in the main pane.

2

A nav that mirrors the mental model. Focus (now/queue, waiting, someday), Log (today, week, done), System. Nothing invented: every section answers a question you actually ask — "what's next?", "what am I blocked on?", "what did I do?"

3

The queue is an order, not a backlog. Items hold an explicit position — position 1 is what Enter starts next. Each held item carries its re-entry point ("resume at: check the decay window"), so starting is never a cold start.

4

Inspector, not modal. Selection drives the right pane: state, scope, re-entry note, sub-tasks, and the note trail. Every field is a flat row — no cards inside cards. Actions repeat at the bottom with their keys, teaching the keyboard as you click.

5

One input, two meanings, zero modes. The command bar captures: plain text while something is selected becomes a note; plain text otherwise becomes a queue item. ⌘K opens search/commands. There is no other way to create anything — so there is nothing to learn.

6

Waiting is a first-class state. Blocked items name what they're waiting on and leave the queue entirely — they can't guilt you from position 3. A daily sweep asks about anything waiting > 3 days: still blocked, or quietly done?
